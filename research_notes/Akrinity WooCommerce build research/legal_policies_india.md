## 1. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, CPA 2019: disclosures, grievance, returns/refunds, made-to-order exceptions, cancellations, COD

### Takeaway
A store with its own website selling its own goods is an "inventory-model" e-commerce entity and is covered by the 2020 Rules (legal name/address/contact/grievance officer disclosures, price breakup, return/refund/warranty terms, country of origin, 48-hour acknowledgement and one-month redress). The 2020 Rules were amended on 9 Sep 2026 (in force 1 Jan 2027): binding dark-pattern compliance with annual self-audit and displayed certificate, "prior price" (lowest in last 30 days) display for discounts, National Consumer Helpline integration. I found no statutory carve-out in the CPA/Rules for made-to-order/personalised goods in the sources reviewed, so a "no returns on custom items" policy is only safely enforceable for change-of-mind returns, not for defective, misdescribed, or non-conforming goods.

### Cited Findings
- The 2020 Rules were notified 23 July 2020 under the CPA 2019 and cover marketplace and inventory models for goods and services sold over digital networks; breaches attract CPA 2019 penalties. (Secondary summaries; the official text is GSR 462(E).) — [Mondaq/LiveLaw-style summaries via search](https://www.livelaw.in/columns/consumer-protection-in-the-online-marketplace-an-analysis-of-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020-161516); [IndiaLaw summary](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- The Rules apply to "all forms of online retail, whether single brand or multi-channel"; inventory entities must display return, refund, warranty, delivery, payment-method and complaint-resolution info plus the total price with breakup of charges (delivery, taxes), and cannot refuse returns/refunds for defective products. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- Entity-level disclosures: legal name, HQ/branch addresses, website, customer-support contacts; importer name for imported goods; grievance officer name/contact/designation shown on the platform. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/columns/consumer-protection-in-the-online-marketplace-an-analysis-of-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020-161516)
- Grievance timelines: acknowledge complaint within 48 hours; redress within one month of receipt (sellers on marketplaces need their own grievance officer on the same timelines). — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/); [SCC Online on the 2026 amendment restating 48h/one month](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/)
- Seller (marketplace) duties incl. Amazon: provide legal name/address, GSTIN/PAN to platform; display price breakup, expiry/best-before, country of origin, importer details, return/refund terms incl. who bears shipping, delivery, warranties; no fake reviews; ads must match the product; must accept returns/refund for defective or misrepresented goods or late delivery (except force majeure). — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- Cancellation charges: an e-commerce entity may not impose cancellation charges on consumers unless it bears similar charges when it cancels. Refunds must be processed promptly under RBI rules (no fixed day count in this source). Consent must be explicit, no pre-ticked boxes. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- E-Commerce (Amendment) Rules 2026: notified 9 Sep 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027, without prior draft; require compliance with the Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023 (13 listed patterns incl. false urgency, confirm shaming, subscription trap, basket sneaking, bait and switch), annual self-audit and prominent display of the audit certificate; the rules do not prescribe audit method/format. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/); [Internet Freedom Foundation critique](https://internetfreedom.in/a-certificate-is-not-compliance-and-the-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-forget-that/)
- 2026 amendment: when advertising a price reduction show the lowest price in the preceding 30 days; prominently display legal name, principal geographic address, website, customer-care and grievance officer contacts; disclose importer details and full country of origin; National Consumer Helpline convergence; provide complainant a copy of the recorded complaint; sellers disclose return/refund/exchange policy, return shipping costs, warranty, delivery, payment modes, best-before dates, and (new Rule 6(5)(j)) GST/MSME registration numbers; marketplace country-of-origin filter from 1 Jul 2027. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)
- CPA 2019 s.2(10) "defect" = any fault/imperfection/shortcoming in quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard required by law, contract, or the seller's own claims. — [Search-result reproduction of the Act (see statute at India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18964/1/cpa.pdf)
- CPA s.2(46) "unfair contract" = terms causing significant change in consumer's rights; commentary says an unreasonably short return window can be treated as unfair. — [Khaitan CPA 2019 note](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/Consumer%20Protection%20Act%2C%202019.pdf); [iPleaders analysis](https://blog.ipleaders.in/analysis-consumer-protection-act-2019/)
- Commentary: refusal to take back defective goods or refund for deficient services is an unfair trade practice (s.2(47)(viii)), and a blanket no-return clause does not override the statute for defective goods. One wiki states a 30-day default refund window if none is stated; I could not confirm it against statute text. — [RTI Wiki consumer rights](https://righttoinformation.wiki/rights/consumer-rights); [legitquest CPA text](https://legitquest.com/act/consumer-protection-act-2019/71B8)
- Secondary source says the Act "removed the practice of displaying 'no refund / no replacement'" slogans. — [BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/law-and-policy/will-the-new-consumer-protection-act-make-consumers-king)

### Inferences
- Akrinity's own WooCommerce store = "inventory e-commerce entity"; the Amazon storefront = "seller on a marketplace". Both sets of disclosures apply, in the respective channel.
- Because 1 Jan 2027 is ~7 weeks away, build the site now with: prior-price logic (if showing "was/now" prices, record 30-day lowest), no countdown timers/false scarcity/pre-ticked add-ons/forced-account checkout/hidden charges (dark patterns), and a plan for an annual dark-pattern self-audit record + certificate/badge. LAWYER: confirm whether the audit/certificate obligation attaches to a small inventory seller and in what form.
- I found no express exception in the reviewed sources for custom/personalised goods (unlike EU/UK law). The defensible approach (general reasoning, not sourced): offer no change-of-mind return/cancellation once production starts on made-to-order items, but expressly preserve remedies for defect, damage in transit, wrong colour/material/dimensions vs the confirmed order/proof, and non-delivery. Define "defect" objectively (print failures, layer delamination, warping beyond stated tolerance, wrong file printed) and state tolerances (e.g., ±x mm, layer lines are inherent to FDM) to align with s.2(10) "required by contract/seller's claims".
- Cancellation for in-production items: a fair, tiered scheme (full refund before production start; refund less material/machine-time actually incurred once printing started; none after dispatch except defect) is consistent with the "no cancellation charges unless seller bears similar" rule only if the retained amount is a genuine cost recovery and is disclosed upfront; LAWYER to confirm the rule's application to deposits.
- COD: prepaid-refund-to-source and COD-refund (bank/UPI) timelines should be stated; RBI TAT norms apply to failed/reversed digital payments; a typical policy is "refund initiated within X (e.g., 7) business days of return approval to original payment mode; COD refunds by bank transfer/UPI" — wording is practical suggestion; no statutory day count found.

### Gaps
- Exact text of Rule 4(4)/(7)/(11) etc. and Rule 6 of the 2020 Rules (rule-number mapping): fetched only secondary summaries; gazette text not retrieved. The Khaitan PDF fetch returned HTTP 500.
- No statutory refund-day count for e-commerce found (sources say "per RBI rules"). RBI TAT circular not retrieved.
- No case law found on "no return" for personalised/custom goods under CPA 2019; no express carve-out found. The s.2(47)(viii) and 30-day refund points rest on commentary, not verified statute text.
- Whether the 2026 amendment obligations (dark-pattern audit certificate, prior price) apply to inventory entities and sellers equally was not clear from sources (India Briefing said it does not state).
- CCPA guidelines on "Prevention of Misleading Advertisements" / "Greenwashing" and CCPA's 2023 dark patterns text not reviewed.
- Whether "Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules" labelling (declarations on pre-packaged goods: MRP, net quantity, manufacturer address, consumer care) applies to packaged 3D-printed goods: not researched. LAWYER/CA.

## 2. Cash on Delivery, prepayment and deposits for custom orders

### Takeaway
No source reviewed prohibits advance payments or deposits for made-to-order work; the constraints come from fairness (CPA unfair-contract/unfair-trade-practice concepts), the 2020 Rules' cancellation-charge rule, and 2026 dark-pattern rules. Non-refundable-deposit clauses are defensible only if tied to costs actually incurred, disclosed before payment, and subject to defect/non-delivery remedies.

### Cited Findings
- E-commerce entities cannot charge consumers cancellation fees unless they bear similar charges when they cancel. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- A contract with terms causing a significant change in consumer rights is an "unfair contract" under CPA s.2(46); commentators say unreasonably short return windows can be caught. — [Khaitan](https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/Consumer%20Protection%20Act%2C%202019.pdf); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/analysis-consumer-protection-act-2019/)
- Refusing to refund for defective goods/deficient services is an unfair trade practice per commentary; blanket no-return clauses do not override this. — [RTI Wiki](https://righttoinformation.wiki/rights/consumer-rights); [legitquest](https://legitquest.com/act/consumer-protection-act-2019/71B8)
- Dark-pattern guidelines (now binding from 1 Jan 2027) cover drip pricing/basket sneaking/subscription traps, i.e., hidden fees or add-ons at the payment stage. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/)
- Under Indian GST, marketplaces collect TCS on net taxable supplies (rate 0.5% since 10 Jul 2024); not directly relevant to COD wording but relevant to Amazon payouts. — [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/gst-registration-mandatory-amazon-flipkart-sellers)

### Inferences
- Suggested structure (practical, not statutory): (1) quote + design proof approval before any payment; (2) 30-50% advance (or full prepay) for custom work, balance before dispatch; (3) deposit refundable in full if Akrinity cancels or cannot deliver, or if customer cancels before work starts; (4) after production/design work starts, retain only a stated, itemised amount reflecting materials, machine time and design hours already spent; (5) a short plain-language box "What happens if you cancel" shown before payment, plus checkbox (not pre-ticked) to accept it.
- Avoid the word "non-refundable" alone; use "the deposit covers work already done and is retained only to that extent"; it signals cost recovery and reduces unfair-contract risk. LAWYER: confirm.
- COD for custom/made-to-order items is risky (RTO losses); practical approach: prepayment-only for custom items, COD only for ready-made stock; show COD fee clearly before checkout (avoid drip pricing).

### Gaps
- No Indian case law or regulator guidance found specifically on non-refundable deposits for made-to-order goods.
- RBI rules on refund TAT and COD refund mechanics not retrieved.
- Whether Amazon's own A-to-z/return policies override seller custom-order terms on the Amazon channel: not researched (Amazon Seller Central policy review needed).

## 3. Intellectual property, platform liability, prohibited items

### Takeaway
Akrinity is not a pure intermediary: it prints and sells goods from files, so it cannot rely on s.79 safe harbour for the physical goods it manufactures/sells; it can only mitigate with intake checks, a rights-warranty and indemnity, a takedown/notice process, and a refusal right. Design ownership should be handled by a written, signed assignment or explicit licence (Copyright Act s.19). Printing weapons/regulated items should be prohibited outright.

### Cited Findings
- Copyright Act s.19: an assignment is valid only if in writing signed by assignor; if duration is unstated it is deemed five years from assignment; if territory is unstated it is presumed India only; s.19(4): rights not exercised within one year of assignment are deemed lapsed unless the contract says otherwise (as quoted from the statute text). — [Advocatekhoj s.19](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/copyright/19.php); [Lawgist s.19](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/19)
- Safe harbour: Shreya Singhal read down s.79(3)(b) so "actual knowledge" requires a court order or government notification (not a private complaint) and unlawful acts tied to Art. 19(2) grounds. — [LiveLaw on Shreya Singhal](https://www.livelaw.in/columns/shreya-singhal-v-union-of-india-offers-us-a-north-star-for-intermediary-liability-154617); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/intermediary-liability-analysis-of-the-effect-of-the-shreya-singhal-judgment-on-internet-intermediaries-in-india/)
- Takedown timing sources conflict: one source says removal within 36 hours of a court order/government notice (with an Oct 2025 amendment effective 15 Nov 2025 per another), while LiveLaw reports 3-hour deadlines for certain government directions; whether private IP notices trigger the clock is contested. — [KS&K on safe harbour](https://ksandk.com/corporate/safe-harbor-intermediary-immunity-indian-law/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/how-it-act-powers-are-killing-online-dissent-528338); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/crime/1652682/the-crumbling-shield-of-safe-harbor-a-legal-critique-of-intermediary-immunity-under-indian-law)
- 3D-printed weapons: commentary says the Arms Act 1959 and Arms Rules 2016 govern manufacture/sale/possession of arms and were built for physical weapons; blueprint dissemination is a recognised gap. No confirmation of a 2025 amendment found. — [LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/articles/3d-printing-blind-spot-arms-control-555491); [Policy Circle](https://www.policycircle.org/opinion/3d-printed-weapons-india-arms-law/amp/); [Arms Act text, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15343/1/the_arms_act%2c_1959.pdf)
- Toys: the BIS Toys QCO applies to toys intended for children under 14, incl. online marketplaces and retailers (see Q5). — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/bis-toy-certification-india-manufacturers-importers-guide-44957.html)

### Inferences (general knowledge, LAWYER to verify)
- Safe harbour (s.79, IT Intermediary Rules 2021) protects intermediaries that host/transmit third-party content without initiating, selecting or modifying it. A shop that prints and sells goods from user-uploaded files "initiates" and "modifies"/profits as a manufacturer, so assume primary or contributory liability for infringing prints. Treat the Amazon listing similarly (Amazon's own IP-complaint rules will apply).
- Practical IP clauses: (a) customer warrants they own or are licensed to the uploaded design and that it does not infringe copyright/trademark/design/personality rights; (b) indemnity for claims and costs; (c) Akrinity may refuse, pause or cancel any order on suspicion of infringement, with full refund of unstarted work; (d) rights-holder notice form + named contact, response commitment (e.g., acknowledge in 48 hours), remove listing/stop production on credible notice; (e) repeat-infringer policy; (f) no printing of licensed characters/brand logos (fan figurines) unless the customer shows written licence; offer to print as "unbranded/original/inspired" only if genuinely non-infringing.
- Ownership of designs Akrinity creates: choose per product. Options: (1) full assignment on full payment (state perpetual, worldwide, all media; add "no 5-year/India-only default" language to avoid s.19(5)/(6) defaults; give Akrinity a portfolio-display licence and retain pre-existing tools/libraries/templates); (2) licence-only (customer gets exclusive/non-exclusive licence to use/print, Akrinity retains copyright). Moral rights (s.57: right to claim authorship and to restrain distortion; text of s.57 not retrieved here) cannot be fully assigned: use credit-line option and customer acknowledgement/waiver-of-enforcement language as far as law permits; LAWYER to confirm enforceability. Assignment effective only upon full payment; until then, licence limited to review.
- Also state: AI-generated/third-party asset components are excluded from assignment warranties; open-source/CC-licensed bases carry their own terms.
- Prohibited-items clause (suggested list): firearms, firearm parts/magazines/suppressors/auto sears, ammunition, bladed weapons/knuckle dusters and other offensive weapons, explosives/devices, lock-bypass/key-duplication tools of protected keys, ID/currency/seal/stamp replicas, skimming/surveillance devices, medical devices/implants/prosthetics/dental aligners and anything making a clinical claim (regulated under Medical Device Rules 2017: not researched), food-contact items marketed as food-safe, children's products presented as safe/certified without testing, counterfeit/branded goods, items infringing IP, hate/obscene/illegal content, and any item the law of destination country restricts. Reserve right to refuse and report.

### Gaps
- Text of Copyright Act s.57 (moral rights) not retrieved.
- IT Intermediary Rules 2021 provisions on copyright/IP complaint handling (Rule 3) and whether the 36-hour vs 3-hour rule is currently in force: unresolved; conflicting sources.
- No sourced treatment of Arms Act s.3/s.5 as applied to "parts" or of BNS provisions; a July 2026 Supreme Court reference in one source could not be verified.
- Medical Device Rules 2017 / CDSCO and Designs Act 2000 / Trade Marks Act 1999 treatment not researched.
- Platform-specific (Amazon) restricted-product and IP policy not reviewed.

## 4. Data protection: DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025, privacy policy, uploaded design files, GDPR phase

### Takeaway
DPDP Rules were notified 13/14 Nov 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)) in phases; the substantive duties (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, retention, children's data) start about 13 May 2027, with consent-manager provisions around 13 Nov 2026. A January 2026 MeitY proposal to cut the 18 months to 12 was floated but I found no notification of it. There is no blanket small-business/startup exemption. Akrinity should draft the privacy policy and consent flows now to the Rule 3 standard.

### Cited Findings
- Phasing: Board-related rules effective on publication (13 Nov 2025); consent-manager rule (Rule 4) at 12 months; Rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23 at 18 months (~13 May 2027). — [Legal 500/Ikigai Law](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [MirvoLegal timeline, as at Sep 2026](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1706890/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Date discrepancies: sources give 12/13/14 May 2027 for the 18-month mark; some firm summaries cite "May 2026", which is inconsistent with the notification date and treated as an error. — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/dpdp-act-compliance-mandate); [Legal 500 on MeitY plan](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)
- MeitY proposed (discussed 23 Jan 2026, comments to 4 Feb 2026) shortening the main window to 12 months and enforcing cross-border Rules 13(4)/15 immediately; no notified amendment found as of the MirvoLegal Sep 2026 review. — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html); [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Data Protection Board: no chair/members reported appointed; MeitY invited applications on 6 May 2026 (per MirvoLegal). — [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Notice (Rule 3): standalone, plain language, itemised data, specified purposes, goods/services enabled, link to withdraw consent, exercise rights, complain to the Board; available in Eighth Schedule languages on request. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Security (Rule 6): encryption/masking/tokenisation, access control, logs retained at least one year, backups/continuity, processor contracts must carry security terms. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Breach (Rule 7): inform affected individuals without delay; inform the Board without delay and give detailed report within 72 hours (extendable); no harm threshold. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Retention/erasure (Rule 8): 3-year-inactivity erasure with 48 hours' notice applies to large e-commerce/gaming/social media above user thresholds; all fiduciaries retain certain personal/traffic data and logs for at least one year; others set deletion policies tied to purpose end. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Children (Rule 10): verifiable parental consent for under-18s' data, with narrow exemptions. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Penalty ceilings per Act's schedule: up to Rs 250 cr for security failure, Rs 200 cr for breach-reporting/children's data failures. — [MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)
- Cross-border (Rule 15): transfers allowed subject to government conditions/restrictions. — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)
- No blanket startup/MSME exemption; s.17(3) is only an enabling power for government to notify classes for relief (from ss.5, 8(3), 8(7), 10, 11), not notified per sources reviewed. — [KS&K DPDP for startups/MSMEs](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-compliance-for-startups-msmes/); [Tsaaro on exemptions](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/regulatory-carve-outs-examining-exemptions-under-the-dpdp-act-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025)
- Previous SPDI Rules 2011 under IT Act s.43A continue until the DPDP duties bite (per Legal 500). — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/dpdp-act-compliance-mandate)
- The 2026 e-commerce amendment bars marketplaces using consumer data for associated-brand promotion without express consent and notes data-fiduciary obligations under DPDP. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)

### Inferences
- Even though main DPDP duties start ~May 2027, SPDI Rules 2011 apply now (reasonable security, privacy policy). Build the DPDP-grade privacy notice and consent logging now; also log consent timestamps/versions; implement deletion and correction request workflow (email-based is fine at start) and a named grievance contact (can double as the e-commerce grievance officer).
- Customer design files: file may contain personal data (names, faces in scans/photos, addresses, personal text/engravings); treat as personal data when it identifies a person, and in all cases as confidential commercial material (confidentiality clause, restricted access, no reuse/portfolio display without permission, retention period e.g., delete X months after order completion unless customer opts to keep, deletion on request, secure storage/access controls, processor/vendor clause for cloud storage and couriers). For under-18 customers, require parent/guardian consent or an 18+ purchasing clause.
- Privacy policy must say (DPDP Rule 3 + e-commerce practice): who we are, what data (itemised: contact, shipping, payment tokens (via gateway), files, device/cookies, support chats), why (specific purposes), legal basis/consent and how to withdraw, who it is shared with (payment gateway, couriers, hosting, Amazon where relevant), cross-border transfer notice, retention periods, security measures, rights (access, correction, erasure, grievance, nominate), how to complain to the Board, children, cookies/analytics, policy-change notice, contact details. 
- International phase: GDPR (EU/UK customers) would add lawful-basis tests, DPA with processors, SCCs/transfer tools, 30-day data-subject request handling, 72-hour supervisory authority breach notice, possibly an EU representative (Art. 27) if targeting EU residents; not researched here: separate review before launching international shipping. DPDP Rule 15/13(4) cross-border conditions could tighten per MeitY plan.

### Gaps
- Official Gazette text (G.S.R. 846(E) vs 843(E) cited inconsistently for rules/Act commencement) not retrieved; verify at MeitY/India Code.
- Whether the 12-month proposal was ever notified after Sep 2026: unknown; check MeitY. Rule 4 start status and the Board's constitution as of Nov 2026: unconfirmed.
- Exact Third Schedule thresholds for "large" e-commerce (to confirm Akrinity is outside Rule 8 3-year erasure): not retrieved (inferred small seller would be below threshold).
- GDPR/UK GDPR requirements: no sources gathered (out of tool budget).

## 5. Business compliance: GST, e-commerce operator rules, Udyam, Shops Act, IEC, BIS toys, material safety

### Takeaway
Selling via Amazon makes GST registration effectively mandatory regardless of turnover (s.24(ix)) unless the narrow Notification 34/2023 exemption applies (intra-state goods only, under threshold, no other registration ground) which a seller who ships interstate cannot use. The Toys QCO applies to products intended for children under 14 and has no carve-out for decorative figurines found in the sources reviewed; adult collectible/display figurines must be positioned and, if borderline, checked with BIS. Avoid food-safe claims.

### Cited Findings
- GST s.24(ix): suppliers via an e-commerce operator collecting TCS under s.52 must register regardless of turnover; Notification 34/2023 (from 1 Oct 2023) exempts goods sellers (not services) with aggregate turnover below Rs 40 lakh (Rs 20 lakh special-category states) making only intra-state supplies, if no other ground applies, declaring PAN/enrolment; any inter-state supply ends the exemption; composition scheme not available to e-commerce sellers; FBA warehousing requires registration in each stock state; TCS 0.5% (0.25%+0.25% CGST/SGST or 0.5% IGST) since 10 Jul 2024. — [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/gst-registration-mandatory-amazon-flipkart-sellers); [Tally on D2C vs marketplace](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-for-e-commerce-sellers-marketplace-vs-d2c-explained/)
- Rs 40 lakh is not a universal goods threshold; it varies by state (Rs 20 lakh in some, e.g., Telangana, Uttarakhand per one source). — [IRIS GST summary](https://irisgst.com/?p=17848); search summary
- Udyam: not legally compulsory for most; micro limit from 1 Apr 2025 is investment Rs 2.5 cr/turnover Rs 10 cr (small Rs 25 cr/Rs 100 cr; medium Rs 125 cr/Rs 500 cr; S.O. 1364(E), 21 Mar 2025); Udyam does not replace GST or Shops & Establishment licence. One source claims Udyam became mandatory from 1 Jul 2020, conflicting with others. — [TaxGuru on MSME limits](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/msme-threshold-limit-effective-1st-april-2025.html); [XFlow MSME guide](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/msme-registration)
- 2026 e-commerce amendment: marketplace sellers must provide/display government-issued ID such as GST or MSME number. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/)
- IEC: still required for export/import compliance; DGFT Notification 36/2026-27 (15 Sep 2026) exempts low-value export consignments (reported as up to Rs 3 lakh per consignment) from RCMC only; IEC, customs, GST not removed. A July 2026 proposal had Rs 10,000 FOB. — [World Trade Scanner](https://worldtradescanner.com/36-Ntfn-DGFT-15.09.2026.htm); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/dgft-proposes-exemption-for-low-value-shipments-up-to-10000); [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/?p=26810)
- Toys QCO 2020 (in force 1 Jan 2021 per most sources): covers toys designed or intended for play by children under 14; non-electric toys must meet IS 9873 series and electric toys IS 15644:2006, with ISI mark; applies to manufacturers, importers, distributors, retailers, online marketplaces; no person may manufacture, sell, store or exhibit for sale toys without ISI mark; penalties under BIS Act 2016 incl. seizure, fine, prosecution. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/bis-toy-certification-india-manufacturers-importers-guide-44957.html); [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/consumer/insight-bulletins/toy-order-2020-approved-india); [BIS blog](https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/BISBlog/toys-quality-control-order/)
- Exemptions found: non-electric toys intended exclusively for export; 2024 amendment added an R&D-import exemption (max 300 units/year, 5 per type, no commercial sale). — [Aleph India](https://alephindia.in/toys-quality-control-amendment-order-2024-for-toy-manufacturers.php); [Intertek](https://intertek.com/products-retail/insight-bulletins/2020/toy-order-2020-approved-india)
- A WTO-centre policy brief defines toys by HS 9503 (including scale models), so scale models fall in the toy definition by default; no collectibles carve-out found (EU excludes "collectors' products", not Indian law). — [IIFT policy brief](https://wtocentre.iift.ac.in/PolicyBrief/Policy_Brief_23.pdf); [Alephindia search result](https://alephindia.in/toys-quality-control-amendment-order-2024-for-toy-manufacturers.php)

### Inferences
- GST: because Akrinity sells on Amazon, get regular GSTIN early (before first Amazon sale); own-site sales are covered under same GSTIN. For custom design services (a service), threshold and place-of-supply rules differ (general knowledge: services threshold Rs 20 lakh/Rs 10 lakh special states, with inter-state services below threshold generally exempted by notification; unverified here): CA to confirm. Invoices need HSN/SAC, GSTIN, place of supply; GST-inclusive pricing shown (price breakup).
- Udyam: voluntary but advisable (free; needed for MSME benefits, now displayed on marketplaces). Shops and Establishments: state-specific; many states require registration/intimation even for e-commerce/home offices: CA/lawyer to check the applicable state Act (not researched).
- IEC: needed to export goods in own name (apply on DGFT, PAN-based, free/low fee). Courier export of e-commerce goods has its own regime (not researched): confirm with CA/customs broker before the international phase.
- BIS toys: figurines/action figures/dolls/play sets/puzzles/building toys/pull-along toys marketed to or likely to be used by under-14s are risky; the QCO tests by "intended for play" as expressed in design, marketing, packaging. Safer product categories: desk organisers, cable holders, planters, phone stands, tools/jigs, home decor, adult hobby miniatures (tabletop gaming miniatures and display figurines) clearly labelled "not a toy / 14+", with no child-oriented marketing. LAWYER/BIS: confirm whether "collectibles/display models for 14+" are outside the QCO; the Order's text was not retrieved. Practical: do not list toy-like items in Amazon "Toys & Games" category (category triggers Amazon's BIS certificate requirement).
- Material safety: avoid claims of "food-safe", "skin-safe", "BPA-free", "non-toxic", or "medical grade" unless tested; FDM layer lines trap bacteria and many filaments (PLA, PETG) and dyes/additives are not food-contact certified (general knowledge). Add a standard disclaimer: "Not for food contact, not a toy for children under 14 unless stated, not for medical use, heat limits (e.g., PLA softens ~55-60 C)." Misleading safety claims also risk unfair trade practice under CPA.

## 6. Policy pages checklist (privacy, terms, shipping, returns/refund/cancellation, warranty, IP/file-handling, grievance)

### Takeaway
At minimum seven pages are needed; the 2020/2026 e-commerce rules mandate visible entity details, grievance officer, return/refund/exchange/warranty/delivery/payment terms, country of origin and price breakup; the DPDP privacy notice has its own Rule 3 content list. I found no source giving verbatim "compliant Indian store" templates to summarise; the structure below is synthesised from the cited rules plus practical convention.

### Cited Findings
- Mandatory on-site entity info: legal name, principal geographic address, website, customer-care and grievance officer contacts (name/designation/contact); importer and country of origin for imported goods; seller GST/MSME number (2026 amendment, mainly marketplace sellers). — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)
- Product-page/checkout info: total price with breakup, return/refund/exchange conditions, who bears return shipping, warranties/guarantees, delivery details, payment methods, expiry/best-before where applicable, country of origin. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/)
- Grievance page: officer name/contact, 48-hour acknowledgement, one-month resolution, copy of recorded complaint to complainant, National Consumer Helpline integration (from 2027). — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)
- Consent: no pre-checked boxes; no dark patterns; display dark-pattern audit certificate from 2027. — [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)
- Privacy notice content per DPDP Rule 3 (see Q4). — [Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025)

### Inferences (suggested page structure; adapt, LAWYER to review)
1. About/Contact/Legal-entity footer block: legal/trading name, proprietorship/LLP/Pvt Ltd status, registered address, email/phone, GSTIN, Udyam no., grievance officer details. 
2. Terms of Service/Sale: eligibility (18+), orders and acceptance, pricing/GST, ready-made vs made-to-order vs design-service terms, quote/proof approval, production timelines as estimates, tolerances/nature of FDM prints, colour variation, payment/advance, cancellation, risk and title, limitation of liability (not excluding statutory consumer rights), governing law India + courts (note: consumer can file in their own district under CPA, don't over-rely on exclusive jurisdiction), dispute resolution, changes to terms.
3. Shipping & Delivery: serviceable PIN codes, dispatch lead times (ready-made vs custom), couriers, charges, COD availability/fee, tracking, damaged/lost parcel procedure (unboxing video, report window), international note "coming soon" with duties/taxes responsibility.
4. Returns, Refunds & Cancellation: three tracks (ready-made; made-to-order; design services), return window and conditions, defect/damage process with evidence and timelines, refund method and timing, COD refund via bank/UPI, deposit treatment, what is excluded and why (custom items: change of mind), statutory rights preserved.
5. Warranty/Quality policy: what "defect" means, tolerances, care/limits (heat, UV, load), no medical/food/child-safety claims, replacement/repair/refund order, duration of defect claim (e.g., 7-30 days from delivery for workmanship).
6. IP, Design Ownership & File-Handling policy: customer upload warranties, prohibited designs, takedown procedure, ownership/assignment or licence per service type, moral-rights credit, portfolio use opt-in, file confidentiality, retention/deletion, rights-holder contact.
7. Privacy Policy (+ cookie notice and consent banner): per DPDP Rule 3 items; grievance contact; retention table.
8. Prohibited Items & Acceptable Use policy (can be a section in Terms).
9. Grievance Redressal page: officer, escalation steps, timelines (48h/1 month), National Consumer Helpline reference, Amazon channel note.
