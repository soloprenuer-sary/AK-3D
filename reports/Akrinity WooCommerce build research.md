# Launch Akrinity lean: shop first, quote engine later

The sourced evidence supports a build that fits under ₹15,000 in year one, but only if three decisions are made early. First, host Phases 1-2 on managed shared hosting (Hostinger Premium, ₹6,672 on a 48-month prepay) and send customer 3D files straight from the browser to a private Cloudflare R2 bucket, which sidesteps the 100 MB Cloudflare proxy cap, PHP limits and nginx limits that would otherwise break 100-300 MB uploads. Second, keep the commerce stack on free plugins (Razorpay, Shiprocket, a GST-capable invoice plugin, a free COD/advance rules plugin) so the only fixed costs are hosting, domain and a few hundred rupees of storage and messaging; the real cost is the **2.36% effective Razorpay fee** and RTO losses on COD. Third, treat the instant quote engine as a Phase 3 Python microservice that needs a small VPS (about ₹7,400/yr on Lightsail's $7 tier), added only once Phases 1-2 are earning. Our estimated year-one cash outlay is about **₹11,600**, rising to roughly ₹14,000 if the Shiprocket Business plan is also triggered, so headroom is thin and the unverified GST treatment of hosting prices could erase it. Legally, two dated deadlines dominate: the E-Commerce (Amendment) Rules 2026 bite on **1 January 2027** and the substantive DPDP duties start around **13 May 2027**. Many figures in the underlying notes are vendor or secondary-source claims; the final section lists exactly which ones remain unverified.

## Shared hosting plus direct-to-R2 uploads keeps hosting near ₹10,000

The hosting evidence is clear on price and murky on limits. Hostinger's 48-month Premium plan costs ₹139/month (₹6,672 total, 20 GB SSD, renews ₹449/month), Unlimited costs ₹199/month (₹9,552, 50 GB NVMe, daily backups, renews ₹649/month), and all plans carry a 30-day money-back guarantee ([Hostinger India WordPress hosting](https://www.hostinger.com/in/wordpress-hosting)). A self-managed VPS removes upload limits but costs more cash up front: Hostinger KVM 1 needs ₹14,376 prepaid for 24 months (renewing at ₹999/month), which alone consumes the whole budget ([Hostinger India VPS](https://www.hostinger.com/in/vps-hosting)), while AWS Lightsail in Mumbai runs $7 for 1 GB or $12 for 2 GB per month ([AWS Lightsail pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/lightsail/pricing/)). At the research assumption of ₹88/USD (not sourced) those are ₹7,392 and ₹12,672 a year. Cloudways starts at $14/month for 1 GB, about ₹14,800/yr, and is out of range ([Cloudways pricing](https://www.cloudways.com/en/pricing.php)). The research ranked a CloudPanel VPS first on control and shared hosting second; we invert that for the first year because a solo developer who is also building a theme and several plugins should not also be the sysadmin, and because the upload problem can be solved at the architecture level instead.

That architecture is the load-bearing recommendation. Cloudflare caps proxied request bodies at 100 MB on Free and Pro plans ([Cloudflare error 413 docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/support/troubleshooting/http-status-codes/4xx-client-error/error-413/)), nginx defaults to a 1 MB body limit ([nginx core docs](https://nginx.org/en/docs/http/ngx_http_core_module.html)), and Hostinger's own tutorial says shared-hosting PHP limits cannot be edited via php.ini ([Hostinger tutorial](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/?p=8078)). Cloudflare's R2 object store supports presigned PUT and multipart uploads with 5 MiB minimum parts, a 5 TiB object ceiling, and automatic abort of incomplete uploads after 7 days ([R2 presigned URLs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/s3/presigned-urls/); [R2 multipart docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/objects/multipart-objects/)). If WordPress only signs short-lived URLs and records metadata, the PHP process never touches the file bytes, so a 300 MB STEP file behaves the same on a ₹139/month plan as on a VPS. Storage is nearly free: R2 gives 10 GB free, then $0.015/GB-month with free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), so 100 GB retained costs roughly ₹1,400 a year by the notes' own arithmetic. Backblaze B2 at $6.95/TB-month is a cheaper fallback ([Backblaze B2 pricing](https://www.backblaze.com/cloud-storage/pricing)). Two caveats are not yet tested: that the browser-to-R2 request truly escapes the Cloudflare proxy cap (the notes infer this from the documented limit and say to confirm by testing), and that Hostinger shared hosting tolerates the signing endpoint and a background validator. Test both inside the 30-day refund window before committing to the 48-month term.

Secure handling of the files follows OWASP and WordPress behavior. OWASP says to allowlist extensions, replace user filenames with random IDs, store files outside the web root or on a different host, require authentication and authorization on every access, and not rely on client-supplied Content-Type ([OWASP File Upload Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/File_Upload_Cheat_Sheet.html)). WordPress rejects unknown extensions through `wp_check_filetype_and_ext()` ([WP Developer Reference](https://developer.wordpress.org/reference/functions/wp_check_filetype_and_ext/)), and WooCommerce itself warns that merely hiding directory listings does not protect confidential upload-field files ([WooCommerce: Hardening File Uploads](https://woocommerce.com/document/product-add-ons/hardening-file-uploads/)). So the design is a custom REST route with a real `permission_callback`, ownership checks on every sign and read request, a private bucket, presigned GETs that expire in minutes, and Cloudflare Turnstile (free, unlimited verifications) on the guest quote form ([Turnstile plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)). The case for a purpose-built endpoint rather than a generic upload plugin is empirical: 2026 advisories include arbitrary-file-upload bugs in WP User Frontend, Slider Revolution and others ([Patchstack database](https://patchstack.com/tag/arbitrary-file-upload); [Wordfence](https://www.wordfence.com/blog/2026/05/authenticated-arbitrary-file-upload-vulnerability-patched-in-slider-revolution-7-wordpress-plugin/)). Block PHP execution under `wp-content/uploads` as a backstop ([WPBeginner](https://www.wpbeginner.com/wp-tutorials/how-to-disable-php-execution-in-certain-wordpress-directories/)). Antivirus is not worth it: ClamAV recommends 3 GiB or more of RAM ([ClamAV docs](https://docs.clamav.net/print.html)), and the realistic risks (disguised executables, zip bombs in 3MF, malformed meshes) are better handled by structure checks and a quarantine-until-validated state. The magic-byte checks (for example the binary STL rule that file size equals 84 + 50 times the triangle count) come from general knowledge, not a fetched spec, and must be checked against the format specifications.

For the 3D viewer, use three.js directly, lazy-loaded only on product and upload pages. `STLLoader` parses binary and ASCII STL from an in-memory buffer ([three.js STLLoader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/STLLoader.html)), which lets the page compute bounding box and volume before upload, while model-viewer is GLB-centred and better suited to showcase models and AR ([modelviewer.dev](https://modelviewer.dev/)). STEP and IGES can be previewed client-side with `occt-import-js`, an OpenCascade WebAssembly build that reads STEP, IGES and BREP and returns three.js-compatible meshes ([occt-import-js](https://github.com/kovacsv/occt-import-js)); load it only when such a file is chosen, and fall back to "file received, preview unavailable" otherwise. STL is unitless, so the UI must make the customer confirm millimetres or inches. Browser-side numbers are advisory; the server must revalidate. Existing viewer plugins such as Easy 3D Viewer put 3MF behind a paid tier and carry the plugin-vulnerability risk above ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/woo-3d-viewer/)).

## The commerce stack is nearly free, but COD and shipping rules decide margins

Razorpay is the default gateway. Its domestic fee is 2% plus 18% GST (about **2.36%**) with no setup, AMC or refund fee, T+1 settlement, and international cards at up to 3% plus GST ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)). The official WooCommerce plugin is free, has 100,000+ installs and was updated on 4 November 2026, but it scores 2.5/5 and shipped fixes in 2026 for an unauthenticated order-value disclosure, a SQL injection in webhook storage and IDOR issues in Magic Checkout endpoints ([WordPress.org plugin page](https://wordpress.org/plugins/woo-razorpay/)). Our recommendation, following the notes, is to leave Magic Checkout off, enable auto-updates and test in sandbox by swapping API keys. Cashfree (1.95%) and Easebuzz (claimed about 1.5%) are cheaper on paper ([Cashfree pricing](https://www.cashfree.com/payment-gateway-charges/)), but the gap is under ₹50 per ₹10,000 of sales, so integration quality matters more than rate. For advance-plus-balance custom jobs, avoid a $99-119 deposit plugin ([PH Deposits](https://woocommerce.com/products/ph-deposits-and-partial-payments/)): take the advance through ordinary checkout and collect the balance with a Razorpay Payment Link that has partial payments enabled ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/payment-links/partial-payments/)), or create a linked second order. Razorpay's KYC for a sole proprietor needs PAN, ID, business proof and name-matched bank proof ([Razorpay documents guide](https://razorpay.com/blog/documents-required-for-payment-gateway)).

Cash on delivery should be switched off for every made-to-order category. Vendor and platform data put COD return-to-origin at roughly 22-38% against 1.5-5% for prepaid, including 37.5% versus 5.3% in ClickPost's FY2026 D2C data and 30.1% versus 1.5% in Qikink's print-on-demand base ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/ecommerce-rto-statistics-india); [Qikink](https://qikink.com/blog/best-payment-gateways-india/)). A returned personalised part is unsaleable inventory plus wasted print time. For stocked catalogue items, COD can stay behind a 20-30% advance, a visible COD fee and pincode restrictions, all available in the free tier of the "Disable payment method / COD fees / Partial payment" plugin, which is HPOS-compatible ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/disable-payment-method-for-woocommerce/)). Note that these RTO figures are vendor data with no official benchmark, and no India-specific number exists for personalised goods.

For shipping, start with the free Shiprocket plugin (pincode check on the product page, white-label tracking, rates from ₹23 per 500 g) and the free Lite plan, which averages ₹45 per shipment and covers up to 5 orders a month ([WordPress.org Shiprocket plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/shiprocket/); [Shiprocket pricing](https://shiprocket.in/pricing)). The ₹199/month Business plan averages ₹41 per shipment, so the break-even is about 33-50 shipments a month. Delhivery publishes no public rate card and added a ₹2 surface surcharge from 1 September 2026 per one source ([PluginHive](https://www.pluginhive.com/delhivery-shipping-charges-hike-for-woocommerce/)). The operational risk for 3D prints is weight disputes: sellers get roughly seven days to dispute, after which extra freight is deducted from the wallet ([Shiprocket weight dispute manager](https://www.shiprocket.in/knowledgebase/weight-dispute-manager)), so weigh and photograph every packed parcel.

GST shapes the invoice template and the Amazon channel. Because Akrinity ships inter-state and will sell on Amazon, it needs regular GST registration from the first sale regardless of turnover: inter-state goods supply requires registration ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-registration-exemption-upto-%E2%82%B940-lakhs-riders.html)), the composition scheme excludes inter-state and e-commerce-portal sales ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-composition-scheme)), and sellers through an e-commerce operator must register under s.24(ix) ([Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/gst-registration-mandatory-amazon-flipkart-sellers)). Own-website sales carry no marketplace TCS, which is 0.5% on Amazon sales since 10 July 2024 ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-for-e-commerce-sellers-marketplace-vs-d2c-explained/)). E-invoicing applies only above ₹5 crore turnover for B2B ([Tally e-invoicing 2026](https://tallysolutions.com/business-guides/e-invoicing-compliance-rules-in-2026/)). The classification questions are the weak point: most sources put 3D printing as a service at SAC 9989 at 18%, but one gives 998347 and none is a CBIC or AAR source ([Sigmatechnik](https://www.sigmatechnik.com/3d-printing-services/demystifying-the-3d-printing-service-sac-code-a-comprehensive-guide)), and rates for plastic articles under 3926 conflict across sources ([Gimbooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/hsn-code-3926-other-plastics-materials-gst-rate/)). For invoicing, the free PDF Invoices & Packing Slips plugin is well maintained (300,000+ installs) but has no native GST fields, while Indian GST Invoice Suite has GSTIN, HSN/SAC and CGST/SGST/IGST splits but only 30+ installs and a warning that it is untested with recent WordPress versions ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/woocommerce-pdf-invoices-packing-slips/); [WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/indian-gst-invoice-suite/)). Since the developer is writing custom plugins anyway, the pragmatic path is the maintained invoice plugin with a custom GST template layer, tested on staging, and itemising the design fee on its own line.

## The quote engine climbs from a manual form to an instant estimate

Competitors set the bar. Zbotic's widget analyses an upload "usually under a minute", shows size, volume and surface area, adds GST at checkout, shows a dispatch estimate, and falls back to an emailed manual quote with the failure reason when analysis fails ([Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-service/)); IamRapid advertises 60-second quotes and lists FDM PLA at ₹6/g ([IamRapid pricing](https://iamrapid.com/pricing/)). Indian per-gram prices span about ₹3-8 for FDM and ₹8-15 for SLA across sources, with minimum orders of ₹200-2,000 ([Zbotic comparison](https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-service-comparison-india-2026-zbotic-vs-a3dxyz-vs-3ding-and-more/)). Those competitors are vendor-written, and the fetched pages show no refund or reprint guarantee, which is a differentiation opening for Akrinity but is an absence-of-evidence finding.

The pricing formula the notes derive from cited inputs is cost-plus: material (grams × waste × price per gram) plus machine-hours (electricity, depreciation, maintenance, with a failure uplift) plus labour plus setup, then margin, complexity, quantity and rush multipliers, then 18% GST. On a worked 100 g PLA part (assumed ₹1,000/kg filament, 10% waste, 9 hours, ₹300/hour labour for 30 minutes, ₹50 setup, 50% markup) that gives about ₹651 before GST and about **₹768 with GST**, roughly ₹6.5/g and consistent with IamRapid's list price. Most inputs there are assumptions from a vendor blog ([Zbotic cost guide](https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-costs-india-calculate-material-and-electricity/)); complexity multipliers, rush percentages and discount tiers have no sourced Indian standard, so they are policy choices. Set a minimum order of ₹200-500 plus a setup fee to survive the ₹3-5/g floor competitors quote. Resin needs a separate model keyed to layer count and Z-height rather than volume, and no Indian resin consumables figures were found.

Technically, the notes support a two-tier architecture. PrusaSlicer and OrcaSlicer both have headless CLIs that slice and export G-code, from which filament grams and print time are parsed out of comments; OrcaSlicer's defaults of 1,000,000 triangles and 300 seconds per plate are ready-made guard rails for a public endpoint ([PrusaSlicer man page](https://dyn.manpages.debian.org/trixie/prusa-slicer/prusa-slicer.1); [OrcaSlicer CLI actions](https://www.orcaslicer.com/wiki/cli/cli_actions)). The `trimesh` Python library loads STL, OBJ and 3MF and reports watertightness, volume and bounding box ([trimesh docs](https://trimesh.org/)). So Phase 3 is a small Python service that validates the mesh, returns an instant volume-based estimate, and optionally queues a real slice, with WordPress calling it over REST and PHP never shelling out to a slicer. Off-the-shelf plugins are weak fits: WP3DP slices remotely with Cura 3.4 and gates per-hour pricing behind a subscription ([wp3dprinting.com](https://wp3dprinting.com)), PrintPrice sends customer files to AWS S3 in Frankfurt ([PrintPrice](https://wordpress.org/plugins/printprice-3d-calculator/)), and the newer Birlo and SliceQuote plugins have fewer than 10 installs ([Birlo](https://wordpress.org/plugins/birlo-3d-print/)). A custom build keeps files in India and under your own data-protection terms. The calibration step (fitting print time against volume and surface area using a few sliced reference parts) is the notes' own suggestion, not a sourced method, and must be validated before any instant number is shown as a price rather than an estimate.

The order pipeline should use real WooCommerce orders plus a custom quote table. Statuses are registered with `register_post_status()` and the `wc_order_statuses` filter, and slugs including the `wc-` prefix are stored in a varchar(20), so longer slugs are silently truncated and drop out of queries ([WooCommerce issue #70266](https://github.com/woocommerce/woocommerce/issues/70266)). HPOS is the default for new stores and plugins must declare compatibility and use only order-object CRUD methods ([WooCommerce HPOS recipe book](https://developer.woocommerce.com/docs/features/high-performance-order-storage/recipe-book/)). An approved quote becomes a pending order whose payment link is `WC_Order::get_checkout_payment_url()`, a pattern secured by the order key ([wp-kama reference](https://wp-kama.com/plugin/woocommerce/function/WC_Order::get_checkout_payment_url)). Store the files, analysis, price-breakdown JSON, file hash and validity in a custom table keyed by quote ID, and create the order only on approval so unapproved quotes never pollute order reports; this hybrid is the notes' inference rather than a sourced pattern. The proposed slugs, all inferred, are below.

| Stage | Proposed status slug | Trigger and notes |
|---|---|---|
| Quote requested | `wc-quote-req` | Form submitted, files validated (Phase 1 manual; Phase 3 auto-estimate attached) |
| Quote sent | `wc-quote-sent` | Price and expiry sent by email and WhatsApp utility template |
| Approved by customer | `wc-quote-appr` | Customer accepts; order created; terms checkbox (not pre-ticked) recorded |
| Awaiting advance | `wc-await-adv` | Order-pay link sent; test Razorpay on the order-pay endpoint first |
| Advance received | core `processing` | Reuse core status |
| Queued / printing / post-processing / QC | `wc-queued`, `wc-printing`, `wc-post-proc`, `wc-qc` | Internal; notify customers only at milestones |
| Ready to ship / balance due | `wc-ready-ship` | Balance collected via payment link before dispatch |
| Shipped / delivered | core `completed` plus tracking meta | Shiprocket tracking synced |
| Side exits | core `cancelled`, `refunded`, `on-hold`; custom expired and reprint | Expired quotes, failed prints |

Status hook names for custom statuses and whether custom statuses are payable on order-pay were not confirmed from official docs, so verify both on staging.

## Channels reward free tools first and a trademark filing early

WhatsApp has two tiers. A free click-to-chat button (Joinchat, 700,000+ installs, with WooCommerce and GA4/Pixel events) needs no API ([Joinchat](https://wordpress.org/plugins/creame-whatsapp-me/)). Order notifications need the Cloud API, which Meta bills per delivered template message since 1 July 2025, with 1,000 free service messages a month and billing for service and in-window utility messages resuming from 1 October 2026 ([Meta pricing docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Secondary sources put India utility messages at roughly ₹0.115-0.145 and marketing at ₹0.86-1.09, so a few status messages per order cost under a rupee before GST ([AiSensy](http://m.aisensy.com/blog/whatsapp-per-message-pricing-update-effective-january-1-2026/); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/how-much-does-india-have-to-pay-for-whatsapp-messages-from-october-1)). A free plugin such as Notiqoo uses the official API but needs a dedicated number that is not also on the WhatsApp mobile app ([WordPress.org](https://wordpress.org/plugins/wc-messaging/)), so the owner's existing business number may not be reusable. Keep promotions out of transactional templates because Meta checks categories ([Baat.ai](https://www.baat.ai/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-india)). Meta also requires accounts to be on INR billing by 31 December 2026 for India sold-to customers.

Instagram and Facebook should be treated as a discovery feed, not a checkout. Meta moved Shops to website checkout in mid-2025 ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/meta-phases-out-native-checkout-facebook-instagram-shops/)), and the free Meta for WooCommerce plugin syncs the catalog plus Pixel and Conversions API ([WooCommerce doc](https://woocommerce.com/document/facebook-for-woocommerce/)). Whether Instagram Shops itself is enabled for Indian accounts is unconfirmed; forum threads say it was restricted ([Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-enable-the-instagram-shop-feature-in-india/107462)), so test early and keep click-to-WhatsApp ads and a link-in-bio as fallbacks. Google free listings are available in India ([Google Merchant help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13427531)); the Google Listings & Ads plugin syncs via the Content API ([WordPress.org](https://uk.wordpress.org/plugins/google-listings-and-ads)); custom-made products without GTIN can use `identifier_exists = no` ([Google help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324478)), though missing identifiers can limit performance, so set your own brand and watch diagnostics.

Amazon.in is where the trademark clock matters. Brand Registry is free but needs a registered or pending Indian trademark; Amazon says pending applications "may be eligible" at its discretion, the government fee is ₹4,500 per class for individuals, startups and MSMEs (₹9,000 otherwise), and registration averages 6-18 months ([Amazon.in Seller Blog](https://sell.amazon.in/seller-blog/how-to-register-trademark-in-india)). Filing early, with a lawyer choosing the classes, is the cheapest long-lead item. For stock sync, paid multichannel tools start around ₹2,000-5,000 a month ([Aaxonix on Zoho](https://aaxonix.com/resources/zoho-inventory-india-ecommerce/); [Evanik vs Browntape](https://www.evanik.ai/evanik-vs-browntape)) and are unjustified at this scale, so use WooCommerce as stock master, update Amazon by inventory file at first, and write a small SP-API script later; the notes did not research SP-API access for India. Never copy Amazon reviews to the site, since a blog reports a cease-and-desist after one seller did ([JustReview](https://justreview.co/blog/can-i-use-amazon-reviews-on-my-website/)); collect verified on-site reviews instead. Amazon.in's policy text on redirecting buyers off-platform and on price parity was not retrievable, so keep website links out of Amazon packaging and messages until Seller Central policy is checked.

For SEO, Google applies no duplicate-content penalty to reused manufacturer descriptions but typically shows one version, so write distinct copy, photos and Product/Offer markup for the site rather than reusing Amazon bullets ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-duplicate-product-descriptions/420899/)). Merchant-listing markup supports shipping and return details ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product)). Beyond that the notes have a real gap: nothing was sourced on Core Web Vitals, Rank Math versus Yoast, Google Business Profile for a workshop business, or Indian keyword volumes. The suggested content plan (a "3D printing service Mumbai" page, PLA versus PETG guides, upload-and-quote landing pages) is general practice, not evidence. Run one structured-data source only, and load three.js only where needed.

## Two dated rule changes and a design-ownership gap drive the legal work

The E-Commerce (Amendment) Rules 2026 were notified on 9 September 2026 and take effect **1 January 2027**: binding dark-pattern compliance with an annual self-audit and displayed certificate, display of the lowest price in the preceding 30 days when advertising a reduction, prominent seller identity and grievance-officer details, and importer and country-of-origin disclosure ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)). The 2020 Rules already require legal name, address, price breakup, return/refund/warranty terms, a 48-hour complaint acknowledgement and one-month redress ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/)). Build the site now without countdown timers, pre-ticked add-ons, forced accounts or hidden charges. The notes found no statutory carve-out for personalised goods, so "no returns on custom items" safely covers change of mind only; defects, transit damage, wrong material or dimension against the approved quote, and non-delivery must remain remediable. The notes suggest defining defect and tolerance objectively, tiering cancellation by production stage and showing a plain "what happens if you cancel" box before payment, but they present these as practical suggestions, and whether non-refundable deposits are defensible under the cancellation-charge rule is a lawyer question.

Data protection has a clear timetable and a design implication. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025, with substantive duties (notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting) starting about 13 May 2027 and no blanket small-business exemption ([Legal 500/Ikigai](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/a-closer-look-at-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-compliance-for-startups-msmes/)). A January 2026 MeitY proposal to shorten the window to 12 months had not been notified as of a September 2026 review ([MirvoLegal](https://www.mirvolegal.com/insights/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-timeline/)). Build DPDP-grade notice, consent logging, deletion workflow and a 72-hour breach procedure now, since the same checkbox-and-log mechanics serve the file-upload form. Customer design files are both confidential commercial material and potentially personal data. A retention rule (for example deleting originals 90-180 days after completion and unconverted quote uploads after 30 days) is the notes' suggestion, and R2 lifecycle rules can expire objects by prefix, though expiry of completed objects was not separately verified.

Intellectual property is the sharpest structural risk. Akrinity prints and sells from uploaded files, so it cannot assume intermediary safe harbour; after Shreya Singhal, safe harbour turns on actual knowledge via court order or government notification ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/columns/shreya-singhal-v-union-of-india-offers-us-a-north-star-for-intermediary-liability-154617)), but a manufacturer that initiates and modifies the output is probably outside it (the notes' inference). The mitigations are an upload warranty and indemnity, a right to refuse, a rights-holder notice contact, and a no-licensed-characters rule. For designs Akrinity creates, Copyright Act s.19 requires a signed written assignment, and unstated duration defaults to five years and unstated territory to India ([Advocatekhoj s.19](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/copyright/19.php)), so the contract must state perpetual, worldwide terms or choose licence-only. The prohibited-items list (firearms and parts, lock-bypass tools, medical devices, food-contact claims) follows from the Arms Act and consumer-law exposure ([LiveLaw](https://livelaw.in/articles/3d-printing-blind-spot-arms-control-555491)). The BIS Toys Order covers toys for children under 14, including online retailers, with ISI marking and penalties ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/bis-toy-certification-india-manufacturers-importers-guide-44957.html)); the note's IIFT source defines toys by HS 9503 including scale models, and no collectibles carve-out was found ([IIFT policy brief](https://wtocentre.iift.ac.in/PolicyBrief/Policy_Brief_23.pdf)). Because the ready-made range already on Amazon.in is unknown to the research, check every SKU against the toy definition before listing it on Akrinity.

The phase mapping below keeps each phase shippable and funds each from the one before. Phase 1 is the smallest thing that takes money legally and safely; Phase 2 adds retention and B2B; Phase 3 adds the engine once order flow justifies the compute.

## Conclusion

The central insight is that the budget constraint and the technical constraint share one solution: moving file bytes off the web server. Direct-to-object-storage uploads make cheap shared hosting viable, make secure storage cost a few hundred rupees a year, and shrink the attack surface to one small endpoint, which is why the plan spends almost nothing on infrastructure until Phase 3 actually needs a compute server. That also reorders the plan: the engine, the glamorous part, is the last thing to fund, and the cheapest long-lead items (GST registration, a trademark filing, policy pages and DPDP-grade consent) are the first.

What remains uncertain is concentrated in a few testable places rather than spread thinly. Four cheap experiments would convert the largest assumptions into facts before real money is committed: a 300 MB upload to R2 from a shared-hosting page with Cloudflare proxying on, Razorpay on a custom-status order-pay link, a sliced reference set to calibrate instant estimates, and a lawyer-and-CA session covering the table above. Until those are done, the ₹11,600 baseline is a defensible estimate and not a commitment, and the plan's largest exposures are policy risks (COD, toys, infringing uploads, Amazon rules) rather than software ones.
